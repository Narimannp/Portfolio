Project 01 / BIM automation

Automated BIM Quantity Takeoff & Evaluation

Object-based quantities with visual traceability—from design geometry to quantities that can be inspected and evaluated.

Model geometryQuantity analysisVisual verificationSoftware architecture
01 / The problem

What a drawing shows is only part of the design.

01 — Visibility

Hidden quantities can be overlooked

Traditional takeoff from 2D plans and elevations relies on what is represented and visible in those views. Hidden surfaces and volumes can be overlooked when the drawings do not make them apparent.

02 — Effort

Manual measurement takes time

Quantity takeoff demands careful measurement and interpretation. Accuracy matters, yet a human error can occur easily and remain difficult to detect.

03 — Verification

A second review has the same vulnerability

Manual takeoff is often followed by another human evaluation. This adds effort, while the review itself remains subject to omissions and calculation errors.

02 / The goal

Make quantities easier to trust and assess.

Create an accurate, object-oriented quantity takeoff process that is easy to assess and verify, using design geometry rather than drawing visibility.

03 / The solution

Extract the quantity. Show its source.

A / Object-based extraction

Use the actual design objects

Collect 3D model elements and extract the required properties and geometry. Quantities are derived from model objects rather than being limited to elements visible in a particular drawing.

The completeness and quality of the source model remain essential to the resulting quantities.

B / Visual evaluation

Make every quantity traceable

Generate color-coded visualizations that map quantity items to their source geometry, including hidden quantities and overlapping layers.

Organize the results by distinct project locations, including units and rooms, so reviewers can assess both individual items and grouped totals.
